# Summary of lecture 'Surveying'
---
## Surveying
### What is surveying?
- science of determining the **position**, in three dimensions, of natural and man-made features on or beneath the surface of the earth.
### Use of Surveying
- contour map, geological map, cadastral map, archeological map
### Primary Classification
1. Plane surveying
   - Not consider curvature of earth since cover small area (less than 100mi^2, 250 km^2)
2. Geodetic surveying
   - consider curvature of earth
   - cover large are greater than (100mi^2, 250km^2)
   - very high precision or accuracy is required
### Surveying Instrument
- Tape, Theodolite, Rod, Prism, GPS, Level, ...
### Types of Survey
1. Topographic surveying: collecting data and preparing maps showing the locations of features and elevation of points
2. Route surveys: long-narrow projects associated with engineering projects (Highways, railroads, pipelinies, ...)
3. Hydrographic surveying: mapping of shorelines and the bottom of bodies of water
4. Boundary surveying: corners, boundaries,...
5. Control surveying: network of horizontal and vertical monuments
6. Photogrammetry: mapping data obtained by carmera or others
### Survey Principle
**Leveling**
- process by which differences in height between two or more points can be determined.
- Benchmark (BM): fixed reference point of known elevation, also called datum
- Back sight (BS): point of known elevation such as on the benchmark or a change point.
- Height of instrument (HI): the elevation of the lin of sight of the telescope
- For sight (FS): the staff reading at a point of unknown elevation
